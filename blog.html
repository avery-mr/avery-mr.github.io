Week 02

2026-05-27

This week I mainly focused on defining the topic and scope of my project. After considering several different possible directions I am leaning toward a curriculum focused on OpenGL fundamentals and shader programming which I feel could help direct my career path towards the more technical side of computer graphics and also give me a deeper understanding of the underlying concepts that power many of the tools I use professionally.

I spent some time setting up my GitHub pages site and experimenting with different themes and layouts. I want the site to serve as both a development journal and a portfolio of work completed throughout CSPB, so in addition to documenting weekly progress, I would eventually like to create dedicated pages for project showcases and links to other GitHub repositories. I am considering building parts of the site directly with HTML and CSS rather than relying entirely on a Jekyll theme but that is certainly not a priority.

I picked up Computer Graphics Programming in OpenGL and spent a little time reviewing other resources like LearnOpenGL and The Book of Shaders. My initial thought was to focus on graphics fundamentals first, understanding the rendering pipeline, transformations, coordinate spaces, and shader basics, and then moving into lighting, materials, and more creative shader development.

One challenge I am finding is that it is rather difficult to build a detailed curriculum and schedule before I have enough familiarity with the material to really judge what can realistically be accomplished within a single semester. I will try to lay out as best as possible a path to a realistic goal, but it may prove to be a bit of a moving target.

Looking ahead to next week, I need to finalize the project scope, establish a rough roadmap, and start working through the introductory OpenGL material. I really want to avoid simply consuming tutorials and actually build small tangible graphics projects as early as possible.

Week 03

2026-06-04

This past week I focused primarily on developing my project proposal and trying to better understand what a semester study of OpenGL and computer graphics might realistically look like. I was eager and tempted to dive in immediately to some of my source material but I decided to spend a little more time trying to map out and understand the major concepts and what could define milestones throughout the semester.

The most challenging aspect of ths so far has been determining how to organize the curriculum around meaningful deliverables, and based on the professor's feedback I've been thinking about the project as a sequence of increasingly complex graphics demonstrations with each milestone resulting in a visible artifact that demonstrates a specific concept. I also spent a bit of time researching generally the field of computer graphics and how OpenGL (as well as other graphics APIs) fit into the overall ecosystem. It has become increasingly clear that of the topics I initially imagined as individual milestones could actually be entire courses on their own, and because of the that I am trying to focus less on mastering every aspect and more on building a solid foundation that can support future learning after CSPB.

Next week I plan to polish out the details of my roadmap and deliverable demos and begin working through introductory OpenGL material. The first practical hurdle will be setting up a stable OpenGL dev environment and deciding which toolchain I will use, configuring libraries, C++ compilers, etc.

Week 04

2026-06-11

This week I focused on my first milestone in OpenGL: setting up the environment. The heavy lift has been getting the basic project structure in place, setting up CMake and understanding CmakeLists, linking GLFW and GLAD correctly (and learning how they fit into the pipeline) and getting my first C++ program to compile and run. The result was getting a window to open successfully which is not as trivial as expected.

Once the window was running I added a callback to handle resizing, added some very basic keyboard input processing, then experimented with simple rendering loop behavior and was able to change the background color. I spent some time learning more about frame buffer clearing and buffer swapping.

This week felt like clearing a first hurdle. It has been a while since I've done anything with C++ so dusting off the cobwebs a bit. Obviously not doing anything complex for a while but I now feel like I have a working foundation where I can compile code, open a window, initialize some OpenGL functions, and see some results. Next step will be to move from simply clearing the screen to drawing my first vertices and triangles diving into shaders and the actual graphics pipeline. It is surreal working throughout the day on highly complex 3d scene files with hundreds of millions of polys and advanced raytracing and global illumination, and then jumping into this project and getting extremely excited about managing to draw a red triangle.

Week 05

2026-06-18

This week I made triangles, and seeing actual rendered geometry felt like a first major milestone. I worked through the process of creating vertex buffers, vertex array objects, and shader programs, and overall got a better understanding of how data is sent to the GPU and processed through the rendering pipeline.

I spent a significant amount of experimenting with variations on the examples to reinforce what I've been learning. I created multiple shapes using a single VAO and VBO, then did the same with separate VBOs for different objects, and experimented with multiple fragment shaders to better understand how different shader programs affect the final image. Really trying to make sure I get beyond just following instructions and exploring as much off the code as possible on my own.

I started diving into the introductory chapters of 'the Book of Shaders' to get a head start on my next milestone. So far it has provided a very different perspective than LearnOpenGL, with more emphasis on the mathematical and creative side of shader development rather than the mechanics openGL.

I decided to rebuild my project portfolio website from scratch using plain HTML instead of continuing with the template I had originally set up, but I'm second guessing my decision now. I thought it might afford me a little more flexibility in design and that I could intentionally forego CSS and visual styling, focusing on building the site's structure and content first. But after seeing so many other great website example of my classmates, it might be better to have something more visually appealing with the ease of a template rather than trying to tackle front end design on top of my openGL project.

One challenge I encountered this week is that OpenGL is already becoming progressively more complex with each chapter, and the learning curve may start getting pretty steep. I have had to devote some extra time this week to studying for my Intro to AI midterm, so hopefully the extra work I put in last week will help me keep up with the curriculum. For next week I plan to continue focusing on shaders, both through LearnOpenGL and The Book of Shaders.
